# Prototype Instructions

## Wheel requirements

These visual constraints apply to the wheel graphic and original static `/wheel` page. Both `/` and `/explore` open the interactive explorer, which adds controls and explanatory text around the same wheel. The reference palette and line values below are defaults; explorer settings may customize the appearance.

- Recreate the supplied Junto Institute reference: six primary, 34 secondary, and 68 tertiary sectors, preserving their order and pastel palette.
- Every emotion label must read radially outward, with its first letter nearest the center. Do not flip left-side labels to keep them upright.
- The wheel itself must be editable code and vector geometry, as explicitly requested.
- Keep the wheel center free of text and omit the title and attribution wordmark from the wheel preview and exports. The static `/wheel` page contains no text outside the wheel; the interactive explorer retains its reading panel and controls.
- Preserve the uncluttered white background, ring geometry, and outward-facing emotion labels.
- Make the six primary emotion boundaries more prominent with 10-unit radial dividers spanning all three rings; keep ordinary sector lines at 4 units.

## Interactive explorer

- Both `/` and `/explore` show the explorer with drag/flick rotation, momentum, and a reading panel for every emotion. Keep the earlier Spin/Reset control row and visible interaction instruction paragraph removed. Remove the under-wheel spin row and visible “Spin the wheel” label. Use only the upright, locally hosted rotate icon in an HTML button over the blank hub, with `aria-label="Spin the wheel"`; keep it outside the spinning SVG so the icon stays upright. Preserve the original static wheel at `/wheel`, with no interactive hub icon.
- Activating the hub button chooses uniformly among the 105 unique emotion names and rotates the chosen sector to the right. Select and highlight it only when that spin completes; interruption must not apply a stale selection. With reduced motion, select immediately. On phones and portrait tablets in the drawer layout, a completed hub spin opens the existing information drawer, and closing that drawer returns focus to the hub button while preserving the highlight. The opening animation and manual dragging must not open the drawer.
- Omit the top header bar, original-wheel link, and divider. Place the existing 28px favicon beside a dark 18px “Department of Emotions” brand above the reading content instead.
- Omit the introductory “A moment to understand” caption and “Find a word for what you feel” heading; keep the wheel and reading panel as the main content.
- Clicking a family button selects its primary emotion and rotates the center of that section toward the right, using the shortest route.
- Place family navigation below the reading content in normal flow, outside the changing emotion content; keep the brand above it outside that content as well so emotion changes do not shift either. Do not position navigation at the top of the page. The selected family has a weight-650 label and a 1px outline around its 9px color dot, offset by 3px; omit pill backgrounds and button borders. Indicate keyboard focus with a text underline. Hide family navigation in the phone and portrait-tablet drawer layout.
- Clip the spinning rotor to the circular stage so it never intercepts clicks on the family buttons. Use non-scrolling clipping so focusing a sector cannot scroll the wheel inside its frame.
- Show the hovered emotion's full hierarchy in one stable breadcrumb row, such as Fear → Scared → Frightened, and retain the primary family's color dot. Do not reintroduce separate “family” or “Related to…” labels.
- Use a white outline by default for the hovered or selected emotion sector; the settings panel may customize it.
- Hovering or focusing an emotion softly fades unrelated sectors while keeping its ancestors and descendants clear. Desktop mouse hover clears on pointer exit. Touch selection keeps its white outline after lift, pointer leave, and blur until another emotion is selected. In the phone and portrait-tablet drawer layout, keep all sectors and stroke layers at their full configured opacity; disable branch fading while preserving selection and the modal drawer. Inline layouts, including landscape screens wider than 600px, retain branch focus. Temporarily remove branch fading during rotation and restore it when rotation stops. Use opacity rather than blur and honor reduced motion.
- In the explorer, default all outer, ring, sector, main, and center stroke widths to 0, retaining editable controls. Use a white 6-unit hover outline, 100% stroke opacity, the source family palette, 100% saturation, 114% contrast, and 97% brightness. These later requested settings supersede the earlier dark dividers; remove the black center-circle outline as explicitly requested. Apply user-selected settings to both explorer routes and custom SVG downloads.
- At desktop widths of at least 1101px, use a full-width main grid with `60vw 1fr` columns. Keep the oversized wheel sticky and vertically centered in the viewport, clipping it at the left, top, and bottom; size it with `clamp(74vw, 120dvh, 92vw)` and place its right edge at `52vw`. Inset the reading column 4vw from its 60vw grid start, placing reading content near 64vw, with a maximum width of 400px.
- At landscape widths from 601px through 1100px, use a legible stacked layout with the wheel up to 780px wide and the reading column up to 400px wide. Keep the reading bands stable as emotions change. Portrait tablets use the phone-style drawer layout instead.
- Use the drawer layout for `(max-width: 600px), (max-width: 1100px) and (orientation: portrait)` in both JavaScript and CSS. Phones up to 600px retain a wheel width of `min(160vw, 820px)`; portrait tablets from 601px through 1100px use `min(120vw, 1100px)`. In both, align the wheel's right edge with the normal content area, then shift it an additional `12vw` left. Center the actual wheel circle vertically at `50dvh` in a viewport-height stage, using `svh` as a fallback. Clip page overflow, including on short screens, without creating a scrolling container. Hide the inline sidebar, family navigation, and page credit footer; retain the rotor's centered spin pivot and keep the hub button visible at the actual hub center despite the left bleed. The modal drawer remains above the stage and scrolls internally.
- In the drawer layout, a confirmed emotion tap or completed hub spin opens its information in a native modal bottom drawer above everything, with a visible “Close” control and the same hierarchy, definition, and reading sections in natural content flow. Limit tablet drawer reading content to 600px wide. Do not open the drawer on initial load, dragging, or arrow-key focus; keyboard activation with Enter or Space opens it. Use the modal top layer, make the background inert, lock page scrolling, allow long drawer content to scroll internally, and respect the safe area. Closing with “Close” or Escape restores focus to the opener and preserves the touch selection highlight. Leaving the drawer-layout query, including rotating a tablet to landscape, closes the drawer and unlocks page scrolling. Landscape screens wider than 600px retain their existing inline layout and fading.
- Show keyboard focus on buttons through subtle fill and border changes, including the drawer’s “Close” button; omit external outline rings. Family navigation uses a text underline instead.
- Stabilize all three inline reading bands: at widths of at least 1280px, give the emotion summary group (hierarchy, title, and definition), somatic section, and response section minimum heights of 216px, 180px, and 352px respectively; at widths from 1101px through 1279px, use 240px, 208px, and 352px; at landscape tablet widths from 601px through 1100px, use 216px, 208px, and 352px. These bands should accommodate the current dataset without shifting the brand or navigation. Allow content to grow for accessibility rather than clipping it. Keep drawer reading content in natural flow without these minimum bands. Reserve stable scrollbar space to prevent wheel shifts and hover jitter.
- Draw the white hover outline above all wheel borders and leave enough SVG padding for its full outer stroke, while preserving circular clipping and the centered rotation pivot.
- Use locally hosted Gilda Display from Google Fonts for the emotion title and retained settings headlines; keep body and wheel labels in their existing sans-serif fonts. The reading-panel section headings “Possible body language & behaviors” and “How it may feel in the body” share the small uppercase sans-serif label style (10px, weight 650, 1.6px letter spacing).
- Hover previews persist so a person can move to the panel and read. Taps, family buttons, and roving keyboard focus also select emotions.
- Keep the “Jump to a feeling” dropdown removed. Beneath the persistent brand, the changing reading content begins directly with the emotion hierarchy and title.
- Combine the earlier “Body language” and “How you might respond” sections into one “Possible body language & behaviors” section. Manually consolidate substantially overlapping examples within each emotion, retaining distinct visible cues and actions. Keep the “How it might show up” heading removed.
- All 108 sectors must resolve to the 105 unique emotion entries. Each entry has a short definition, a curated `responses` list combining body-language and behavior examples, and two `somaticExperiences`; repeated names share the same content. Show “How it may feel in the body” first, followed by “Possible body language & behaviors,” as always-visible sections without carets or collapse controls.
- Each small uppercase reading-panel section heading is followed directly by its numbered examples. Keep the helper descriptions “Sensations you might notice inside your body.” and “Ways someone might act or respond.” removed.
- Keep spacing within the inline reading bands compact while retaining their minimum heights to stabilize section positions across emotions. This supersedes the earlier instruction to avoid reserved space beneath short definitions. Drawer content retains natural spacing without reserved band heights.
- End the emotion reading content after “Possible body language & behaviors,” with family navigation below it. Keep the “Every body is different…” footer note and “About these examples” disclosure, including its research links, removed from the visible interface.
- Include a small “Site created by Jules” credit link to https://www.haggard.design/ in a full-width page footer after the main content, centered across the entire page at the document bottom in normal flow. Use 16px top, 24px side, and 24px bottom padding. Keep the credit outside the wheel controls, reading sidebar, and wheel SVG. Hide the footer in the phone and portrait-tablet drawer layout. Preserve the text-free static `/wheel` page and exports.
- Examples are illustrative, vary by person/culture/context, and must never claim to diagnose or reliably identify a feeling from appearance.
- Write examples as concise, neutral descriptions without repetitive “You may” or “You might” introductions. Prefer natural descriptive phrases rather than commands or claims that everyone experiences an emotion identically.
- Respect reduced motion and keep the wheel usable on narrow and short landscape screens.
- On each initial explorer load, gently rotate the wheel from -60 degrees to its original orientation over 1.4 seconds with an ease-out stop. Skip this opening motion for reduced-motion preferences. Pressing, dragging, selecting a family, or keyboard input must be able to interrupt it immediately; ordinary rerenders must not replay it.

## Wheel settings

- Hide the “Wheel settings” button and editor on both interactive routes, as explicitly requested. Retain the editor source and the approved appearance defaults and saved preferences.
- Provide separate width, color, and opacity controls for the outer border, ring lines, sector lines, main separators, center outline, and hover outline. Keep sliders and color swatches visually distinct.
- Include family palette controls that retain relative sector tints, plus saturation, contrast, brightness, label color, label size, and center fill controls.
- Include hover-focus enablement, muted opacity, fade duration, and a temporary preview. Honor reduced motion regardless of the chosen fade duration.
- Save appearance preferences locally in the browser, recover safely from missing or invalid saved values, and keep the controls usable when storage is unavailable. “Reset all” restores the defaults.
- “Download SVG” exports the full vector wheel with the selected appearance, selectable outward labels, and blank center. Remove transient selection outlines, branch fading, and app controls from the download. Keep export dimensions padded enough for the full outer stroke.
- Preserve the locally hosted upright rotate icon in the accessible hub button; keep its visible label and under-wheel row removed. Do not reintroduce the former top-level control row or instruction paragraph. Keep the static `/wheel` page and SVG exports free of the interactive icon, with a blank hub.

Run the local server yourself and open the preview in the browser available to this environment. Do not give the user server-start instructions when you can run it.

Before making substantial visual changes, use the Product Design plugin's `get-context` skill when the visual source is unclear or no longer matches the current goal. When the user gives durable prototype-specific design feedback, preferences, or decisions, record them in `AGENTS.md`.

When implementing from a selected generated mock, treat that image as the source of truth for layout, component anatomy, density, spacing, color, typography, visible content, and hierarchy.

Build app UI in `src/`. Keep `.openai/hosting.json`, `worker/index.js`, `scripts/prepare-sites-build.mjs`, and `tests/sites-worker.test.mjs` intact so the same local prototype can be handed to Sites. Before a Sites handoff, run `npm run build` and `npm run test:sites`; the build must leave `dist/client/index.html`, `dist/server/index.js`, and `dist/.openai/hosting.json`.

The intended Sites public domain is `departmentofemotions.com`. Treat publication and domain connection as deployment state to verify; do not infer that the site is live from repository configuration alone.
